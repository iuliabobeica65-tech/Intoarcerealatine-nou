The 7 universal laws: what do they mean and how do I use them?

Do you wonder why you repeat certain reactions in relationships, why criticism hurts or why saying no is so difficult? In Echilibrarea rădăcinilor, I use seven universal laws as reference points for reflection on the specific situations you experience.

They are not scientifically proven laws of nature or compulsory explanations for what happens to you. They are tools for formulating questions, not diagnoses about you.

We begin with you and the situation that hurts.

Imagine saying yes to someone again, although you need rest. The next day you are upset: ‘Why must I do everything?’

We begin with facts: what actually happened, what you understood about yourself and when you began seeing things this way. We look for the root in your story, attentive to what truly emerges.

If a relevant experience emerges, we explore perceptions around the observable action, look at opposites through appropriate questions and then check the meanings formed. We return to the initial situation and today's reaction. We do not assume a single moment explains a woman's whole life. Conscious creation begins with this exploration process and the choices you try out.

What are the seven universal laws?

Below is a brief introduction to each reference point, with a situation to explore and a separate article where I go into more detail.

LAW 1 / 7

The Law of Duality

Why do I feel selfish when I say no?

We begin with a specific situation and explore what you considered good or bad in an action. Questions about the opposite help you check whether your initial perception leaves room for other real aspects.

A question to explore: Choose a yes said against your needs. What did it help you with and what did it cost? What did you anticipate would happen if you refused?

LAW 2 / 7

The Law of Reflection

Why do criticism or admiration affect me so much?

A trait seen in someone else can become a label: ‘She can, I cannot.’ We begin with observable behaviour and explore, without forced conclusions, how you relate to the same trait.

A question to explore: Note a trait you strongly admire or judge. What did the person actually do? Is there a comparable action in your own experience?

Read the dedicated article →

LAW 3 / 7

The Law of Synchronicity

During a difficult period, what support existed?

We explore challenges and forms of support that coexisted, without inventing help where none existed or minimising painful experiences.

A question to explore: Describe a manageable challenge. What help did you need? What did you actually receive and what was missing?

Read the dedicated article →

LAW 4 / 7

The Law of Transformation

Who am I when a role or chapter changes?

We look at changes, what ended and what remains important to you. Transformation does not mean denying loss or being obliged to find something good in it.

A question to explore: In a change you can explore safely, what ended? What still matters to your values?

Read the dedicated article →

LAW 5 / 7

The Law of Fractals

Why do I react the same way at home, at work and in relationships?

We notice similarities between different situations. We do not assume they all have the same root or make you responsible for others' behaviour.

A question to explore: Choose two events from different contexts. What did you actually do, what did you anticipate and what is similar, if anything?

LAW 6 / 7

The Law of Order

Why do I have time for everyone, but not myself?

We explore values and priorities through how you use time, energy and resources. There is no universal order imposed on everyone.

A question to explore: Look at the last seven days: what did you actually prioritise, and what matters to you but keeps being postponed?

LAW 7 / 7

Eristic escalation

When desire becomes pressure

We explore situations in which a goal becomes tied to a verdict about personal worth. The aim is not to give up the wish, but to see more clearly what you choose.

A question to explore: If the goal is not achieved, what do you believe that would mean about you? What is fact and what is interpretation?

Frequently asked questions about the universal laws

Do I have to believe in all seven laws?

No. You can use the questions as reflection exercises without accepting spiritual or scientific explanations you do not share. In a meeting, we use only reference points appropriate to your situation.

Does applying the universal laws mean always thinking positively?

No. We do not minimise injustice or try to turn a painful loss into a compulsory positive story. Sometimes the useful conclusion is that you need a clear boundary, support or a practical decision.

Are the universal laws the same as family constellations?

No. The seven laws are reference points used to explore perceptions in Echilibrarea rădăcinilor. Family and systemic constellations are part of how I work in Echilibrarea rădăcinilor and I use them according to context to explore a polarised root more easily.

Can I work with my sense of worthiness?

Yes, for situations such as difficulty asking, receiving and accepting appreciation, I created ‘Your Sense of Worthiness – 4 Weeks for You’, through Echilibrarea rădăcinilor. We begin with your experiences, rather than labels about how much you are worth.
