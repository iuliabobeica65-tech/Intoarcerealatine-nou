The 7 universal laws: what do they mean and how do I use them?

Do you wonder why you repeat certain reactions in relationships, why criticism hurts you, or why it is so difficult to say “no”? In Echilibrarea rădăcinilor (Root Balancing), I use seven universal laws as points of reflection for the real situations you experience.

These are not scientifically demonstrated laws of nature, nor are they compulsory explanations for what happens to you. They are tools for asking questions, not diagnoses about you.

We begin with you and the situation that hurts.

Imagine saying “yes” to someone again, even though you need rest. The next day you are upset: “Why do I have to do everything?”

We begin with the facts: what actually happened, what you came to believe about yourself, and when you began seeing things this way. We look for the root in your own story, paying attention to what genuinely emerges.

If a relevant experience emerges, we explore perceptions connected to the observable action, examine opposites through appropriate questions, and then check the meanings that formed. We return to the original situation and to your reaction today. We do not assume that one moment explains a woman's entire life. Conscious creation begins alongside this process of exploration and the choices you test.

What are the seven universal laws?

Below you will find a brief introduction to each point of reflection, a situation to explore, and a separate article where I go into more detail.

LAW 1 / 7

The Law of Duality

Why do I feel selfish when I say “no”?

We start with a specific situation and explore what you considered good or bad about an action. Questions about its opposite help you check whether your initial perception leaves room for other real aspects.

Question to explore: Choose a time when you said “yes” against your own needs. How did that help you, and what did it cost you? What did you expect would happen if you refused?

LAW 2 / 7

The Law of Reflection

Why do criticism or admiration affect me so deeply?

A trait you see in someone else can become a label: “She can do it, but I can't.” We start with observable behaviour and explore, without forcing conclusions, how you relate to that same trait.

Question to explore: Write down a trait you strongly admire or judge. What did the person actually do? Is there a comparable action in your own experience?

LAW 3 / 7

The Law of Synchronicity

During a difficult period, what support was there?

We explore challenges and forms of support that coexisted, without inventing help where there was none and without minimising painful experiences.

Question to explore: Describe a challenge that feels manageable to explore. What help did you need? What did you actually receive, and what was missing?

LAW 4 / 7

The Law of Transformation

Who am I when a role or chapter changes?

We look at changes, at what has ended, and at what remains important to you. Transformation does not mean denying a loss or being required to find something good in it.

Question to explore: In a change you can safely explore, what has ended? What still matters in relation to your values?

Read the dedicated article →

LAW 5 / 7

The Law of Fractals

Why do I react the same way at home, at work, and in relationships?

We notice similarities between different situations. We do not assume they all have the same root, and we do not make you responsible for other people's behaviour.

Question to explore: Choose two events from different contexts. What did you actually do, what did you anticipate, and what is similar, if anything?

LAW 6 / 7

The Law of Order

Why do I have time for everyone but myself?

We explore values and priorities through how you use your time, energy, and resources. There is no universal order imposed on everyone.

Question to explore: Look at the last seven days: what did you actually prioritise, and what matters to you but keeps getting postponed?

LAW 7 / 7

Eristic Escalation

When desire becomes pressure

We explore situations in which a goal becomes tied to a judgement about personal worth. The aim is not to give up the desire, but to see your choices more clearly.

Question to explore: If the goal is not achieved, what do you believe that would mean about you? What is fact, and what is interpretation?

Frequently asked questions about the universal laws

Do I have to believe in all seven laws?

No. You can use the questions as reflection exercises without accepting spiritual or scientific explanations you do not share. In a session, we use only the points of reference that suit your situation.

Does applying the universal laws mean always thinking positively?

No. We do not minimise an injustice or try to turn a painful loss into a story that must be positive. Sometimes the useful conclusion is that you need a clear boundary, support, or a practical decision.

Are the universal laws the same as family constellations?

No. The seven laws are points of reference for exploring perceptions in Echilibrarea rădăcinilor. Family and systemic constellations are part of how I work within Echilibrarea rădăcinilor, and I use them according to the context to explore a polarised root more easily.

Can I work on my sense of worthiness?

Yes. For situations such as difficulty asking, receiving, and accepting appreciation, I created “Your Sense of Worthiness — 4 Weeks for You”, through Echilibrarea rădăcinilor. We begin with your experiences, not labels about how much you are worth.
