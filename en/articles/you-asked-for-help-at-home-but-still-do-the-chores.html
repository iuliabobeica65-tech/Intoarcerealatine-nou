A situation from everyday life

You Asked for Help at Home, but You Still Do the Chores

2026-10-23 · Iulia Bobeica

It is evening. There are still things to do at home, and you have already said you are tired and need support. You have discussed this several times. For a while, you thought things would change, but tonight you are still taking care of everything.

‘How many more times do I have to ask?’

You have already asked. Why does it hurt so much that nothing changes?

Perhaps it is not only the number of chores that hurts. Perhaps it hurts that you explained, asked and tried to organise things together, and now feel your words did not matter. Or perhaps tiredness simply weighs on you. We do not choose the answer in advance.

Simply telling you ‘learn to ask for help’ is not useful here. You did ask. It is worth looking at what happened afterwards: what you requested, how the other person responded, what was done and what remained your responsibility.

Asking for support does not mean you have to obtain it on your own

Needing help does not make you responsible for others' responses. Sometimes there are misunderstandings about sharing tasks; at other times, the request was very clear but no change followed. These situations are not identical.

What hurt most in the last discussion? What did you understand about yourself when you saw you were still doing the chores? Your answer matters more than an explanation prepared in advance.

How we explore in Echilibrarea rădăcinilor

We begin with the observable action, what you experienced and your conclusions. We explore relevant experiences and perceptions through balancing questions, without assigning blame or promising that others will change. Conscious creation is present from the beginning, as you clarify what you want and which choices are yours.

This article differs from the situation in which you turn down offered help: here you asked for support, and the problem is that you did not receive it.
