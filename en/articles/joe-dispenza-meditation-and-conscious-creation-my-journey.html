Joe Dispenza, Meditation and What I Discovered About Conscious Creation

I have studied Joe Dispenza's work for over eleven years. I do not mention this as a qualification or to suggest my method belongs to him. I mention it because it forms part of my learning journey and how I began looking at conscious creation.

I have always been interested in understanding things deeply. In not stopping at the first explanation. In viewing the same situation from several perspectives, especially when one alone seems to occupy all the space.

You can understand very well what is happening and still react the same way

You have an idea you want to express. You prepared and know what you want to say. But before speaking, a question arises: ‘What if I make a mistake? What if they criticise me?’

You stay silent. Later, after the moment has passed, you find all the right words. You promise yourself you will have courage next time. Yet in a similar situation, you find yourself silent again.

This is a situation for exploration, not a client's story. I use it because it shows the difference between knowing what you want and being able to notice, in real life, what you do when a challenge arises.

I am not interested in telling you in advance why you stayed silent. Perhaps it meant something different to you from what someone else might assume. I am interested in what actually happened, what you understood about yourself and how you came to see the situation that way.

The place of Joe Dispenza's work in my journey

In his work, Joe Dispenza discusses habits, beliefs, meditation and changing how we relate to our experiences. These themes nourished my interest in conscious creation.

But for me, study did not mean only reading about change. I practised Joe Dispenza's meditations. Awareness of space was an important aspect of my experience.

I am not presenting a scientific explanation of meditation here, or a universal conclusion about others' experiences. I am sharing what I understood from my own practice.

Awareness of space and how I viewed polarised perceptions

When I practised the meditations, awareness of space helped me look differently at polarised perceptions: the tendency to remain caught in one side of an experience, in a single meaning assigned to it.

For me, an important connection emerged here between meditation, conscious creation and balancing perceptions. Not because meditation is itself the same procedure as the balancing I use today. Nor because I could claim that simply becoming aware of space automatically changes a life situation.

But because I began looking more closely at what happens when I no longer remain fixed exclusively on a single interpretation.

An experience you call ‘negative’ can occupy so much attention that other perspectives become difficult to notice. This does not mean denying pain, justifying what someone did to you or forcing yourself to see everything positively. It means leaving room for exploration without deciding in advance what you must discover.

This is my personal interpretation of the practice, not a statement attributed to Joe Dispenza or a component automatically adopted into my method.

The support I was seeking and what I began noticing about myself

On the About Me page, I shared another part of my journey: I was looking for help, support and appropriate information. I found fragments, but not always the support I felt I needed. I had many fears, including fear of making mistakes.

At one point, I also began exploring the drawbacks of waiting for support exclusively from outside. I noticed that sometimes I limited my potential, doubted myself and relied on help that might not come.

I did not conclude that support from others was wrong. I began understanding that I can receive support and, at the same time, be on my own side.

For me, this distinction still matters: it is not about choosing between two extremes, but exploring what you could not see when looking at only one of them.

Where this journey meets Root Balancing

Root Balancing is my method, with its own way of working. It is not a method created, taught or certified by Joe Dispenza.

We begin with the present problem, the situation that hurts now. We explore what actually happened, what you understood about yourself and the experiences in which you came to think this way. We do not assume a single cause, and I do not offer my conclusions in place of yours.

In balancing, we work on the observable action through questions from the universal laws used in my method, then check interpretations and what you notice differently. Family and systemic constellations are a distinct way of exploring, used according to context.

Conscious creation does not begin after transformation. It is present from the beginning. I am interested both in how you see the experience and what you begin choosing and doing now.

ReGeneration and systemic constellation training with Anca Maftei also mattered in my study journey. I acknowledge them as influences and sources of learning, without presenting their materials as my own validated components of the method.

More than a beautiful explanation: the life you return to

You may understand why you stayed silent in a discussion. You may talk about courage, fear and what you want. But what do you notice when the next situation in which you have something to say arises?

That is where I want us to return: to your responses, your choices, what changes and what still returns. If a situation repeats, we explore it again. We do not turn a new perspective into a promise that everything will be different from now on.

For me, conscious creation means participating ever more attentively in how you build your life, without claiming you can control others' choices or every circumstance.

Studying Joe Dispenza remains an important part of my journey. Root Balancing, however, remains my method, distinct from these sources, and how I apply it is checked in each person's specific experience.
