When You Feel You Must Hold the Family Together

2026-10-23 · Iulia Bobeica

In some families, one person seems always to know who needs a call, who is upset and what should be done to keep things well. Perhaps they do it with love. Perhaps, sometimes, it is tiring. I cannot know what it means to them simply by looking at their behaviour.

A situation we can look at differently

Imagine a woman who calls her parents almost daily, helps them with errands and tries to reconcile her siblings when a conflict arises. This is a hypothetical example, not a client's case.

At one point she says, ‘If I do not step in, nobody does anything.’ I do not begin by telling her she has the role of rescuer, carries an ancestral burden or learned this in childhood. I ask what happened the last time she stepped in, what each person did and what conclusion she drew.

What a systemic constellation can offer

In a constellation, we use symbolic representations to look at relationships, positions and how a person experiences their place in the family. Emotions, words or perspectives they had not considered may emerge. These are experiences of exploration, not evidence of what other people felt or of hidden family events.

In the systemic approach, belonging, place and the balance between giving and receiving are traditional reference points. I do not present them as scientifically proven laws or assume every present-day problem comes from their being violated.

Where my work with perceptions comes in

If the woman says ‘I am not allowed to stop’ or ‘Without me, the family falls apart’, I do not supply an explanation. We explore which specific situations lie behind those words and what they mean to her. When we work with universal laws, balancing focuses on the observable action, and we then check whether the associated meanings have changed.

Family and systemic constellations are a distinct way of working, used according to context and with the person's consent. A symbolic representation does not automatically establish the cause of a difficulty or replace exploration of facts and perceptions.

You do not need to give up your family to begin noticing which choices are yours and which deserve a closer look.

If you feel it would be useful to learn about this way of working, you can read about constellation meetings.

Before naming a role, we look at an event

When you say you hold the family together, what was the last situation in which you stepped in? Who did you call, and what followed? Have you tried not stepping in? How did you feel?

A symbolic representation does not prove that earlier generations passed a role down to you.

When you find yourself between two loved ones again

Your mother asks you to speak to your sister after an argument. You have already tried to reconcile them, but the conflict returns and you feel responsible for stepping in again. What exactly did each person ask of you? What did you do, and what happened afterwards? We do not assume you inherited a role; we begin with your experience. If you recognise this situation, you can also read the article about being the family peacemaker.
