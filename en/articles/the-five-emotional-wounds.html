The Five Emotional Wounds: What Happens When You No Longer Feel Good Enough?

You prepared a proposal for Monday's meeting. You reread it several times and tell yourself it is ready. When your turn to speak comes, you postpone: ‘Perhaps I should present it next time.’ After the meeting, it hurts that the idea remained unspoken again.

Perhaps you tried writing down your arguments, rehearsing at home or asking a colleague's opinion. Yet in front of others, the same hesitation arises. What exactly happened at that moment? What did you understand about yourself? We do not assume the answer is one of the five ‘wounds’, or that a past experience automatically explains the present.

What are the five emotional wounds?

Personal development literature describes the model of the five emotional wounds: abandonment, betrayal, rejection, humiliation and injustice. Some people use these words to describe experiences and reactions they recognise in their lives. They are not clinical diagnoses and do not mean every behaviour has a single cause.

The abandonment wound is a term used when fear that someone important might leave or no longer be available occupies a large part of a relationship.

The betrayal wound may express pain around broken trust, an unkept promise or infidelity.

The rejection wound may describe moments when we interpret a refusal or criticism as confirmation that we are not good enough.

The humiliation wound is a way of naming experiences in which shame and fear of being exposed become burdensome.

The injustice wound may describe situations in which comparison, unequal treatment and the need to prove something about yourself remain very strong.

And the masks?

The same kind of literature also uses ‘masks’ as a metaphor for behaviours through which we try to protect ourselves. You may say you can manage alone, although help would be useful. You may become very attentive to others' reactions or avoid speaking so as not to be criticised.

I am not interested in telling you which ‘mask’ you wear. I want us to discover together what actually happens, what you do, what you believe would happen if you chose differently and what you have come to believe about yourself.

How I work with these situations in Root Balancing

In Root Balancing, we begin with what hurts now: the specific event, observable action and your conclusions. We explore relevant experiences through questions for balancing perceptions and check what changes, without deciding a cause or label for you.

Depending on the context, family and systemic constellations can form part of the exploration. They do not establish diagnoses or prove what family members felt or intended.

What once helped you adapt may no longer be how you want to choose today. This is precisely what we explore: what you understood then and what you can consciously choose now.

You can also read about Root Balancing or family and systemic constellations.

Conscious creation, as I approach it, is also visible in what you choose to do differently as you balance your perceptions. Not only in what you understood about the past.
