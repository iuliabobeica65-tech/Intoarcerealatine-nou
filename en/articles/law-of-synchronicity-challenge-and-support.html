The Law of Synchronicity: What Did I Not See in a Difficult Moment?

Sometimes we say of a moment, ‘Nobody supported me then.’ Perhaps we really were left without the help we needed. Perhaps we remember very clearly who was absent.

I do not want to contradict such an experience by saying, ‘You must have received support somewhere.’ First, we need to understand which specific moment we are talking about and what support means to the person who lived through it.

Support and challenge, on the same theme

In the material on the Law of Synchronicity, the exploration is very precise: we do not look for the opposite side anywhere and at any time, but at the same moment and on the same theme. This is a personal development perspective, not proof that every second contains measurably equal amounts of support and challenge.

Imagine a hypothetical situation: on an important day, a woman learns that her sister cannot come to help her care for an ill parent. I will not tell her that ‘the Universe sent her someone else’. I will ask her to describe the day, the help she requested and what she actually received. If another relevant form of support exists, she will recognise it herself. If it does not, we do not invent it.

How this may relate to a constellation

The perception ‘I carried everything on my own’ may emerge in a systemic constellation. I do not conclude that someone in the family excluded her or that an ancestor passed a role down to her. I listen. If she asks to continue, we can use questions to explore what actions took place and how she perceived them.

Looking at another side of a moment does not mean forgetting who was absent, erasing suffering or pretending the help received was enough.

In situations of recent loss, grief or danger, we do not rush this exploration. Safety and appropriate support matter.

If you are interested in how I use these questions, I share more on the Root Balancing page.

What happened at that moment?

If you say you had no support, which day and which specific need are you referring to? Who was there, and what did each person do? Did you notice an opposite action on the same theme, at the same moment?

We do not assume support existed. If you cannot identify it, we do not fabricate evidence.

What did you ask for, and what response did you receive?

Perhaps you asked someone to help with a specific task and they could not. What exactly did you ask? What response did you receive? What did you try afterwards? We explore facts and perceptions without assuming there was hidden support. If the difficulty arises when someone actually offers to help, read the article about turning down support.
