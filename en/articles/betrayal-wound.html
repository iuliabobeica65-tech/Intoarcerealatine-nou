The Betrayal Wound: When an Important Promise Is Broken

You agreed to discuss significant expenses before spending shared money. Then you discover by chance that your partner has already made a major purchase. When you ask, they say they wanted to avoid a difficult conversation.

When the issue is not just money

Perhaps you have talked about what happened. You asked why you were not told and how you would make decisions in future. You agreed to discuss future expenses, but you notice yourself checking the finances more often.

Perhaps it hurts because a shared agreement was broken. Perhaps it is difficult to trust a new promise. Or perhaps the main problem is the lack of transparency about a decision affecting both of you. We do not assume your response is excessive or that trust must return on a particular timetable.

What happened, and what did you conclude about yourself?

What was the original agreement? What did you discover, and how? What explanation did you receive? What did you ask to change? What did you conclude about yourself when you learned the promise had been broken? What have you tried, and what has not changed?

“Betrayal wound” is a term used in some personal-development approaches, not a diagnosis. A broken promise is a fact that needs to be distinguished from its personal meanings. Exploring perceptions does not justify withholding information or breaking agreements.

Exploring this through Root Balancing

We begin with the concrete action and your conclusions. We explore perceptions through balancing questions without inventing causes or deciding for you what to do in the relationship. Conscious creation is present from the beginning as you clarify what matters to you and what conditions you need to discuss.

This article concerns a financial agreement, not infidelity. If you would like to explore your own situation, learn about individual sessions or view online appointment availability.
