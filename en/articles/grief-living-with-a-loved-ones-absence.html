When Someone Dear Dies, How Do You Live with Their Absence?

You are setting the table and feel like putting out another plate. Or you hear a song that reminds you of the person you have lost. For a few moments, their absence feels very present again.

How it may show up

Sometimes you avoid places or objects; at other times you seek them out. You may feel longing, anger, numbness or even moments of joy followed by guilt. Reactions differ from person to person.

What it costs you

You may feel you must carry on for others, while there is no space for your own pace of grieving.

What you may have understood about yourself

Some people come to believe ‘If I feel joy, it means I have forgotten’, ‘I should have done more’ or ‘I am no longer allowed to want something for myself’. These thoughts are not truths, nor does everyone experience them.

How it can affect your present

Such meanings can make returning to important activities or relationships more difficult. Grief must not be rushed, and work with perceptions does not replace psychological or medical support when needed.

How we explore through Root Balancing

We begin with what you are experiencing now, explore the associated experiences and perceptions, and check what you understood about yourself. We do not assign you a cause in advance or promise guaranteed results. We work through balancing questions and observe what becomes different in how you view the situation and in your choices.

If you would like us to talk about what you are experiencing, you can learn how individual sessions work. For a first conversation, you can request a free initial 30-minute consultation.

Personal development is not a substitute for medical or psychological treatment. No outcome is guaranteed.
