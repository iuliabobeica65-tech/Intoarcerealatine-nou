Why Do You Feel Ashamed to Be Seen as You Are?

In a meeting, you have an idea. You prepare the first words in your mind, but when a suitable moment to speak arises, you stop: ‘Perhaps it is silly.’ A little later, someone else says something similar and is heard.

When silence hurts more than a negative response

We do not know how others would have reacted to your idea. We know only what happened: you chose not to say it, although you wanted to participate. Afterwards, you think about what you could have said.

Perhaps you tried preparing better. You rehearsed the sentence in your mind, looked for arguments and told yourself you would speak next time. Yet when attention turns towards you, you feel like staying silent again.

Shame, fear of judgement or something else?

Calling this experience ‘shame’ may be useful if that is how you recognise it. For someone else, it may be fear of mistakes, an unsafe group situation or simply needing time to formulate an idea. We do not turn a scene into a diagnosis.

What hurt more: the idea remaining unspoken, seeing it received well when someone else said it or the conclusion you drew about yourself?

What did you understand about yourself when you chose silence?

Return to the specific moment. What did you notice around you? What did you tell yourself? When have you experienced something similar? Your answers, rather than an explanation prepared in advance, show us what deserves exploring.

How we work in Root Balancing

We begin with the painful situation, what you understood about yourself and relevant experiences. We work with perceptions through balancing questions and check what changes. Conscious creation is present from the beginning, including as you clarify what you want to express and how you want to choose.
