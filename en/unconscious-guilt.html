UNCONSCIOUS GUILT · ONE-MONTH PROGRAMME

A mistake can last a moment. But how long do you keep paying for it?

Perhaps you made a choice you would make differently today. You tried to repair it. Time passed. But when you remember, you still tell yourself, ‘I should have done things differently.’

Sometimes, beyond the mistake, a conclusion about yourself remains: ‘I am guilty. I am not good enough. I must make up for it.’ That conclusion deserves a closer look.

A QUESTION THAT CHANGES PERSPECTIVE

You made a mistake. But does that say who you are?

‘I made a mistake’ describes something you did. ‘I am guilty and must pay’ is already a conclusion about yourself.

Perhaps you have carried it for a long time. Perhaps you strive never to disappoint anyone again, demand more of yourself than of others or find accepting the appreciation you want difficult.

Not every such situation means unconscious guilt. That is precisely why the programme begins with your facts and experiences, rather than a label.

The question we begin with:

What actually happened, and what did you come to believe about yourself at that moment?

SITUATIONS YOU MAY RECOGNISE

When the mistake is past, but judgement continues

WITH YOURSELF

You no longer allow yourself mistakes

A decision that did not turn out as you hoped can become another reason to tell yourself you are not good enough.

IN RELATIONSHIPS

You feel you must repair everything

You take on more than your share of responsibility just so things can be well between you again.

IN YOUR LIFE

Enjoying things is difficult

You achieved something, but your mind returns to what you could have done better.

AT WORK

You compensate by always doing more

You work, solve things, help. Yet at the end, the feeling that it is not enough returns.

IN CHOICES

You seek others' approval

You have a clear wish, but doubt your choice until someone important confirms it is all right.

WITHIN YOURSELF

You follow a standard that is not your own

You admired someone, adopted their rules and judge yourself harshly whenever you do not follow them.

These situations are examples for reflection. They are not a test and do not indicate a particular cause on their own.

YOUR ONE-MONTH PROGRAMME

A space to look differently at what you experienced

I created this programme as a starting point for working with perceptions of yourself. For one month, we explore unconscious guilt through Echilibrarea rădăcinilor, with worksheets and support group meetings.

WORKSHEETS

Questions that go beyond ‘I made a mistake’

Materials for looking at specific situations, the conclusions you drew about yourself and perspectives you may not have considered.

GROUP MEETINGS

Support in exploring your own perceptions

A guided setting in which we discuss situations, questions and different ways of viewing experiences, respecting each participant's pace.

What are we working towards?

Distinguishing what happened from what you understood about yourself, noticing how you relate to that experience today and being able to view your choices from a more balanced perspective.

ROOT BALANCING

We do not change the past. We explore how you perceive it.

The work begins with a present situation that is real for you. Through the method's questions, we explore the conclusion you drew about yourself, moments when you felt this before and perspectives that may contribute to a more balanced perception.

  1. We begin with what hurts now. A specific situation, rather than a general label.
  2. We explore what you understood about yourself. Including standards you came to consider compulsory.
  3. We work with perceptions. We use balancing questions adapted to the situation, without assuming the answer.
  4. We return to the present. We notice how you see the situation, what matters to you and which choices you want to make.

Conscious creation is present from the beginning of the process, through how you clarify your wishes, values and choices.
