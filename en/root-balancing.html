ECHILIBRAREA RĂDĂCINILOR

Echilibrarea rădăcinilor — Root Balancing

Discover the root of the patterns holding you back and begin consciously creating a new reality.

You have tried to change what hurts. You have talked, explained and asked for help. And yet, in certain situations, you find yourself back at the same point.

Behind recurring reactions, we gently and carefully explore experiences, perceptions and the conclusions you have drawn about yourself.

WHEN YOU RECOGNISE YOURSELF IN WHAT YOU ARE EXPERIENCING

You know what you want. But the same pain keeps returning.

You are tired of being strong. Of taking care of everyone, carrying responsibilities and promising yourself that you will look after yourself too, when you have time. You have asked for support and explained how tired you are, but once again you end up feeling alone with everything.

You want love, but closeness comes with the fear of being left. You want to trust, yet after a betrayal you catch yourself checking and expecting to be disappointed again.

You want to make your own decisions, but you seek other people's approval. When they decide for you, it hurts that what you want does not seem to matter. You know you need rest, but when you try to choose yourself, guilt appears.

“Why, if I know what I want, do I keep doing the things that hurt me?”

Some pain goes much deeper

Discover the experiences you recognise. Open each topic at your own pace.

Love, relationships and betrayal

You have experienced infidelity and lost your trust. You feel rejected or unseen in a relationship. After a divorce or separation, you struggle with the feeling that you have failed. You fear being abandoned and find it difficult to leave relationships that hurt you.

You find yourself wondering why you are not chosen, appreciated or loved in the way you wish.

Loss and deeply painful experiences

You have lost someone dear to you, and the world kept moving while you were still trying to understand what had happened. You have experienced the loss of a child, a miscarriage or an abortion, and you carry pain, questions or guilt that are difficult to talk about.

You are dealing with infertility, repeated hopes and disappointments. Every loss has its own story and deserves to be met with respect and gentleness.

Family, belonging and family history

You have a difficult relationship with your mother or father. Your family has experienced conflicts, separations, secrets, excluded relatives or painful events that were rarely discussed. You feel responsible for everyone's peace and try to reconcile, help and fix things.

You struggle with feeling that you do not belong or that you must always prove something to be accepted. In systemic exploration, we also address themes such as adoption, abuse, losses, addictions and transgenerational experiences.

Guilt, shame and personal worth

You compare yourself with others and see them as better than you. You receive a compliment and play it down. You have achievements, but find it hard to enjoy them. You want more, but fear you do not deserve it.

You have read, learned and tried to change something, yet in important situations the same reactions return: fear of judgement, helplessness, submission, lack of confidence or the feeling that you are not enough.

WHEN PAIN HAS A NAME

Five emotional wounds, five different experiences

Sometimes you speak of the wounds of abandonment, rejection, betrayal, humiliation or injustice. These are terms used in personal development, not diagnoses. What matters is what you actually experienced and what you came to believe about yourself.

BEYOND SYMPTOMS

What have you come to believe about yourself?

After betrayal, rejection or loss, some people begin to see themselves through conclusions such as “I am not enough”, “I do not deserve to be loved” or “I have to manage on my own”.

Over time, such perceptions may appear in relationships, choices and recurring patterns. We explore what actually happened, the meaning you gave the experience and how you recognise it today.

HOW WE WORK

A deep process, at your own pace

We explore the root of the pattern

We begin with what you are experiencing today and discover the experiences in which you formed conclusions about yourself. We observe how they appear in the present.

We balance and integrate

We work with balancing questions based on the universal laws: duality, reflection and synchronicity. Depending on the context, I also use family and systemic constellations as a distinct way of exploring.

We consciously create a new reality

As we work with perceptions, you clarify your values, desires and choices. Conscious creation accompanies the entire process; it is not merely a final stage.

FAMILY AND SYSTEMIC CONSTELLATIONS

We also explore deep experiences from family history

In constellations, themes may arise such as the loss of a child, abortion, abuse, infidelity, the exclusion of family members, adoption, secrets, grief or transgenerational experiences.

Constellations offer perspectives for exploring relationships, belonging and family dynamics. What emerges is checked together with the person against their own experience, without treating interpretations as proven causes.

Depending on the context, we continue with honouring and integration in systemic work, and with balancing perceptions through the universal laws.

We work in depth, with care, respect and gentleness towards painful experiences.

A root is not always the first thing we discover.

We begin with what hurts today. But as we explore, we may discover that the present problem is connected to several perceptions and conclusions about yourself.

One question may lead to another question. One experience may bring another moment in your life to your attention. A conclusion may appear in relationships, in how you express your needs or in the choices you make. We do not assume they all have the same origin.

Through questions based on the universal laws, we also explore the opposite, without denying what you have experienced or imposing another interpretation on you. We balance the visible action and then check its meanings.

Identifying a root does not simply mean finding an explanation. We look for the clarity you recognise and the changes you notice in everyday life. If the difficulty reappears, we continue exploring.
