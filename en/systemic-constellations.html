A space where your story is heard with respect.

Systemic constellations offer a space for symbolic exploration of relationships, family patterns and situations such as conflicts, separation, grief or questions of belonging. They are a distinct way of working, used according to context, not an automatic explanation of the causes of your experiences.

1. We begin with your intention

I ask what you want to explore during that meeting. We clarify the topic and a few relevant facts about your relationships or family, without trying to analyse your entire story or work on several intentions at once.

2. We choose the representatives

In a group, the person working on their topic may choose participants, with their consent, to represent family members, themselves or other elements important to the situation. We usually begin with a small number of representatives.

3. We position them in the space

The representatives are placed in relation to one another. We observe positions, distances, reactions and how participants describe what they experience in the exercise. These observations are material for exploration, not evidence of hidden events.

4. We look together at what emerges

We symbolically explore relationships and positions without assuming that the representation proves hidden facts. I clarify details from the person's account without offering conclusions.

5. We conclude and integrate

When a constellation helps identify polarisation at the root, we continue with balancing questions from the universal laws. We then check the mind's interpretation.

6. The constellation and balancing perceptions

I use constellations to explore a polarised root more easily, depending on context. My questions during exploration clarify only what happened. For balancing, I use only questions from the universal laws, focused on the visible action. During balancing, we also engage in conscious creation.

Can I participate without working on my own topic?

Depending on the meeting format, you may participate as a representative or observer. Taking on a role is voluntary, and your boundaries are respected.

How do we use constellations?

Constellations are part of my method of working, and I use them according to context. This does not mean every meeting will include a constellation; in a group, every role is voluntary.

What should I know beforehand?

Come with the situation that concerns you and your intention for the meeting. The process differs from one person to another; it does not guarantee discovering a hidden cause and does not replace medical or psychological care.
