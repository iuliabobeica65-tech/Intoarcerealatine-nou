SYSTEMIC CONSTELLATIONS · EXPLORING THE ROOT

A space where your story is heard with respect.

Systemic constellations offer a space for symbolically exploring relationships, family patterns and situations such as conflict, separation, grief or questions about belonging. They are a distinct way of working, used according to context, rather than an automatic explanation of the causes of your experiences.

1. We begin with your intention

I ask what you want to explore in that meeting. We clarify the theme and a few relevant facts about your relationships or family, without trying to analyse the whole story or work on several intentions simultaneously.

2. We choose representatives

In a group, the person working on their theme may choose participants, with their consent, to represent family members, themselves or other elements important to the situation. We usually begin with a few representatives.

3. We position them in the space

Representatives are placed in relation to each other. We notice positions, distances, responses and how participants describe their experience of the exercise. These observations are material for exploration, not evidence about hidden events.

4. We look together at what emerges

We symbolically explore relationships and positions without assuming the representation proves hidden facts. I clarify details in the person's account without offering conclusions.

5. We conclude and integrate

When a constellation helps identify polarisation at the root, we continue with balancing questions from the universal laws. We then check the mind's interpretation.

6. Constellations and balancing perceptions

I use constellations to explore a polarised root more easily, depending on context. My exploration questions clarify only what happened. In balancing, I use only questions from the universal laws, on the observable action. During balancing, we also engage in conscious creation.

Can I participate if I do not want to work on my own theme?

Depending on the meeting format, you can participate as a representative or observer. Taking a role is voluntary and your boundaries are respected.

How do we use constellations?

Constellations are part of my way of working and I use them according to context. This does not mean every meeting includes a constellation; in a group, every role is voluntary.

What should I know beforehand?

Come with the situation concerning you and your intention for the meeting. The process differs from person to person; it does not guarantee discovering a hidden cause or replace medical or psychological care.
